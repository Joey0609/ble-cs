One frequency leaves many possible distances

Separate single-tone ambiguity from the wider ambiguity associated with frequency spacing. Because the propagation path is counted twice, increasing the one-way distance by half a wavelength adds one full phase turn. A single phase angle cannot tell us which cycle we are on. Sweeping frequencies changes the accumulated phase at a rate related to distance. This is why Channel Sounding gathers observations over multiple RF channels. Absolute starting phase, hardware bias and oscillator offsets are additional reasons to work with a compensated two-way measurement instead of a single receive phase.
