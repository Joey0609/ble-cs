Location becomes valuable when it changes an action

Use the three examples to show why tracking is an engineering requirement, not merely an attractive visualization. For asset retrieval, room-level location may be sufficient; for a spatial interaction, delay and consistency can matter more. A track supports analysis of route and dwell, but location data about people should be collected with clear purpose and consent. Ask the audience to name the error or delay that would change a decision in their own application. This makes later tradeoffs in airtime, filtering and geometry tangible.
