20. RTT is a nanosecond problem

Interactive prediction: increase the turnaround while holding the distance fixed. The initiator's total elapsed time increases, but the corrected propagation time and range stay fixed. Then increase distance and observe the much smaller nanosecond change. At three metres, round-trip flight time is about 20.01 ns. A one-nanosecond error after subtraction creates about 0.15 m of range error. The application decodes time_difference in 0.5 ns units; quantization is not a promise of corresponding accuracy. Use exact c internally and rounded display values. This demonstration is a mathematical model, not captured hardware timing.

24. Tiny frequency errors can become range bias

The two tone observations do not occur at the same instant. Residual oscillator frequency difference rotates the combined phase during their separation. Since a fractional offset is proportional to RF frequency, the error can also tilt the fitted slope. Work through the numbers: c times 12e-6 times 69e-6 divided by two is about 0.124 m. The app explicitly says the reported-offset sign convention is not pinned down in its educational analysis and exposes a sign control. Do not claim the selected correction is universally correct. 31. Zoom in: procedure → event → subevent → step

Start with the smallest unit: a step is an RF exchange with one mode on a channel. A subevent groups steps within a reserved time budget. Events organize subevents, and a procedure groups events into a ranging operation. Repeated procedures supply measurements over time. These are CS events; distinguish them from the ACL connection events that anchor scheduling and carry control or data transfer. The app offers separate views for connection, procedures, events and subevents, individual steps and channels. Channel Sounding · 56:45–58:30

34. Channels: coverage and coexistence

The CS channel arrangement differs from the ordinary BLE data-channel grid. Within 2402–2480 MHz, seven reserved indices leave 72 permitted channels. The application can edit the channel map and overlays reference Wi-Fi bands for context. Its example random generator is deliberately not the CS security DRBG; the actual on-air sequence cannot be inferred from the offline example seed. Losing channels can reduce frequency coverage and complicate slope fitting. For ideal uniformly spaced 1 MHz samples, the nominal two-way phase ambiguity interval is c divided by two delta-f, about 150 m; this is not an operating-range guarantee and gaps can make unwrapping harder.

35. Antenna diversity creates paths, not extra anchors

A path is a transmit/receive antenna pairing. Multiple paths offer diversity against poor orientation or fading, but do not automatically become independent surveyed anchors. The specification permits several antenna arrangements subject to implementation capability. The local repository includes an nRF54L15 Tag reflector with two switched antennas; do not assume that this Nordic Tag has the same antenna arrangement as a SensWear board. Channel Sounding · 60:15–62:00

36. Security checks and measurement quality answer different questions

Explain security at a conceptual level rather than presenting this prototype as a secure-access product. Bluetooth CS includes mechanisms intended to reduce opportunities for range manipulation: protected setup, cryptographic randomization and packet/tone checks. RTT and PBR can supply complementary evidence. The exact protection depends on the chosen security level, supported features and how the application consumes indicators. NLOS bias is a propagation problem, not necessarily an attack. Likewise, an attacker can target a clean-looking estimate. Our educational app should be used to inspect observations and validity, not to certify access-control behavior.

Channel Sounding · 62:00–63:45

37. Indoors, the shortest path may be the hardest to see

Point to the direct path and a longer reflected path. The receiver sees a superposition of complex contributions, so phase can curve with frequency rather than follow a single line. If the direct path is suppressed, a dominant longer path can produce a persistent range bias. Averaging helps random noise but does not necessarily correct that bias. Other practical contributors include oscillator residuals, antenna delays, body shadowing and bad report pairing. The most useful teaching experiment is to change one condition—such as orientation or an obstacle—and compare the raw evidence before and after.

57. Two observables, different sensitivities

Preview the two measurement principles before deriving them. RTT cares about extremely small differences between timing intervals; PBR turns variation over frequency into delay. PBR is attractive for fine ranging, but phase ambiguity and multipath matter. RTT provides a complementary check and participates in security designs. The app's separate mean, median and slope traces make these distinctions visible; they are not a mandated Bluetooth fusion algorithm. Ask the audience which observable they expect to change when the reflector processing delay changes.

58. RTT: remove the time spent inside the reflector

Read the four timestamps in order. The initiator measures t4 minus t1 on its clock. The reflector measures t3 minus t2 on its own clock. Subtracting the turnaround removes the time when a signal was not in flight. Divide the remaining round trip by two, then multiply by propagation speed. This model assumes reciprocal propagation and compatible calibrated time scales; it does not require comparing absolute clock epochs. Real implementations also address frequency offset, timestamp bias and packet quality. Host arrival timestamps are not substitutes for controller measurements.

59. Phase tells us where we are in a cycle

Explain frequency, wavelength and phase using the plotted sinusoid. Frequency is cycles per second, wavelength is the spatial length of a cycle, and phase is position within that cycle. A receiver has a local oscillator; measured phase is relative to that reference. We cannot infer a unique metre-scale distance from the fractional position within one radio cycle. The plot is a spatial teaching sketch, not an oscilloscope trace from the application. Introduce radians now so the later slope equation has familiar units.

Reference · Supplementary

60. One frequency leaves many possible distances

Separate single-tone ambiguity from the wider ambiguity associated with frequency spacing. Because the propagation path is counted twice, increasing the one-way distance by half a wavelength adds one full phase turn. A single phase angle cannot tell us which cycle we are on. Sweeping frequencies changes the accumulated phase at a rate related to distance. This is why Channel Sounding gathers observations over multiple RF channels. Absolute starting phase, hardware bias and oscillator offsets are additional reasons to work with a compensated two-way measurement instead of a single receive phase.

61. Pair the two phase observations

Use complex notation only as much as needed: z equals I plus jQ, and its angle is the measured phase. In the conventional two-way exchange, combining matched phase observations cancels the opposing initial oscillator phase terms under the model assumptions. The application's baseline computes the complex product, not the conjugate product. The minus sign follows its receive-phase convention; other conventions may flip the sign. Residual frequency error and hardware effects remain. Peer data are therefore needed for this baseline analysis. Inline PCT Transfer is a newer extension with a different data path and is kept out of this baseline derivation.

62. Distance is the slope across frequency

Differentiate the ideal phase model with respect to frequency. The intercept drops out, leaving minus four pi times range divided by c. The app groups repeated observations by channel, averages complex products for a chosen antenna path, unwraps the phases and fits a line. Do not average wrapped angles arithmetically. Fitting across more well-spaced frequencies can help constrain the slope, but it does not guarantee removal of reflected paths. Explain that sign conventions must be consistent through data decoding, correction and range estimation; avoid silently taking an absolute value to hide a sign error.

The planner includes switching periods and an extension slot, including reserved duration when a transmission is absent. The app excludes that extension slot from normal antenna-path measurements. 67. Mode 3: combine timing and phase in one step

It provides a convenient way to examine timing and phase evidence from related exchanges, at a different airtime cost from tone-only steps.

70. Appendix · a stalled run has a next diagnostic step

Use as an instructor reference during the hands-on session. Begin with observable state rather than changing several settings simultaneously. A command timeout can close the app transport to avoid confusing a late reply with another command; reconnect to inspect the actual client state. If the UI is unresponsive, preserve any saved capture and reduce live analysis work before diagnosing RF issues. Do not treat a simulator run or successful firmware build as hardware verification.

71. Appendix · later Bluetooth features stay explicit

This slide avoids mixing specification versions. The application help references Core 6.3, while some planner details cite Core 6.1. Its IPT option changes reflector tone behavior and switching timing. In that mode, the initiator can receive a two-way phase contribution through the modified exchange, and the app has specialized validation and initiator-only analysis behavior. These are not assumed in the Bluetooth 6.0 baseline derivation. Consult the app's IPT technical note and the relevant current standard before enabling it.
