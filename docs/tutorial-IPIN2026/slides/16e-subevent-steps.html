Inside a subevent: a sequence of steps

Mode 0 frequency and timing calibration; one to three open every subevent
Main mode the measurement steps, here mode 2: phase of tones
Sub mode inserted between main mode steps, here mode 1: round-trip time
Every step initiator to reflector, then reflector to initiator
Core · Vol 6, Part H, §4 (CS steps and mode sequencing)

Zoom into one subevent: it is a sequence of CS steps, and a step is the smallest unit, one exchange on one channel in which the initiator transmits to the reflector and the reflector transmits back. Every subevent opens with one to three mode-0 steps, which calibrate frequency and timing between the two radios. After them come the measurement steps of the main mode; here mode 2, the tone exchange for phase-based ranging. At configured intervals a sub-mode step is inserted, here mode 1, a packet exchange for round-trip time. A procedure uses at most two non-mode-0 modes, and only some pairs are allowed; the mode sequence slide lists them. The configuration can also repeat up to three main-mode steps from the end of the previous subevent right after the mode-0 steps. Each step uses its own channel, chosen by the channel selection that CS security seeded, so the radios retune between steps. The specification allows 2 to 160 steps in a subevent and 256 in a procedure. This sequence is the example the specification itself uses: two mode-0 steps, five mode-2 steps and one mode-1 step; the channel numbers are illustrative. What happens inside each mode is the subject of the mode timing slides.
