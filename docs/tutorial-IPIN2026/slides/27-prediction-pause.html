Let’s think it through

1. Doubling the tone steps gives more samples. What happens to range update rate?
CS airtime and result traffic grow. The procedure may need more events, reducing complete ranges per second.
2. PBR reads 1 m too far with little spread; RTT is noisier but near the reference. What do you check?
Check accepted steps, quality flags, phase residuals and calibration. A repeatable result can still be biased.
3. Rotating the reflector changes the range at a fixed distance. How would you isolate the cause?
Hold the configuration fixed; repeat each orientation and compare phase shape, antenna path, quality and RTT.

Allocate roughly forty seconds per question: brief individual prediction, discussion with a neighbour, then reveal. For question one, ask where extra steps and result bytes fit in the CS and ACL schedule; more samples do not guarantee a higher rate of complete procedures. For question two, ask what each method contributes and why a narrow spread does not establish accuracy. Use the measured reference, validity and quality flags, phase residuals and calibration context before choosing a result. For question three, ask for a controlled follow-up experiment: keep distance and configuration fixed, repeat orientations and inspect both the observations and the range estimates. The exact cause is a hypothesis to test, not something the first range change proves. These questions close the theory parts and prepare the hands-on experiment.
