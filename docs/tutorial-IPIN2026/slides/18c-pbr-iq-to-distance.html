Signs: \( \Theta[k] = \angle H[k] = -4\pi f_k d/c + 2\pi\varepsilon f_k\,\Delta t \); a leftover offset \( \varepsilon > 0 \) (reflector fast) adds phase over the tone gap \( \Delta t \). Correct: \( H[k]\,e^{-j 2\pi\varepsilon f_k \Delta t} \).
With multipath \( H(f_k) = \sum_\ell a_\ell\, e^{-j 4\pi f_k d_\ell / c} \): the shortest \( d_\ell \) is the true distance, so the slope alone is biased and the first path must be found.

In Bluetooth CS the reflector and the initiator each report a complex phase correction term, PCT, per channel and antenna path. Multiplying the two gives H[k], whose angle is the two-way phase Theta. It is the plain product, not one term times the conjugate of the other: the propagation phases add to minus 4 pi f d over c, and the oscillator phases, which have opposite signs, cancel. A conjugate product would do the reverse: cancel the distance and keep the oscillator offset. Multiplying the complex values also avoids unwrapping each measurement on its own. Before estimating, remove the hardware loop phase with a calibration and make sure Mode 0 has corrected the frequency offset: both look exactly like a change of distance. The frequency term has a sign. The reflector measures first and the initiator one tone gap, delta t, later. If the reflector's oscillator runs fast, epsilon positive, the two drift apart by plus 2 pi epsilon f delta t in between, which works against the propagation lag, so the range reads short by c epsilon delta t over 2. The correction multiplies H[k] by e to the minus j 2 pi epsilon f delta t, which subtracts that phase. Epsilon here is only what is still in the reported phases. In CS the initiator has already retuned by the offset it measured, so what is left is the difference between the measured offset and the reported compensation: a fraction of a ppm, positive in one subevent and negative in the next. Removing the full measured offset a second time puts the whole bias back. The calibration section defines epsilon. In a clean line-of-sight channel the slope fit is the best estimator. With reflections, H is a sum of paths and the slope is pulled towards them. An inverse FFT gives the channel impulse response, and the first significant peak is the direct path. Super-resolution methods such as MUSIC or ESPRIT separate paths closer than 2 m. Finally, the 150 m ambiguity is resolved with the RTT measurement from Mode 1 or 3.
