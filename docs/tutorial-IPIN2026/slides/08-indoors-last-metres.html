WHEN + WHERE
Every phenomenon happens at a time and a place. Sensing, actuation and applications all need both, explicitly or implicitly.
OUTDOORS

INDOORS
Satellite signals stop at the roof, yet we spend 87% of our time in buildings (US survey).
Indoors the clock still works, but the position does not: each building needs its own answer.
Klepeis et al., NHAPS, J. Expo. Anal. Environ. Epidemiol. 2001

Open the section with a show of hands: who has tried positioning inside a lab, hospital, warehouse or campus? Then start from first principles: every phenomenon we observe, from the weather to a fall or a meeting, happens at a time and a place, so a sensor reading is only useful with both. The same holds for acting on the world and for the applications on top: a light switched in the wrong room, or an alert at the wrong time, is wrong even if the rest is right. Sometimes the need is explicit, a coordinate and a timestamp; often it is implicit, as in "the device near me, now". Outdoors, GNSS gives position and time together almost anywhere under open sky, which makes it the natural starting point. Indoors the clock still works, from the network, but satellite signals are weak and blocked by roofs and walls, so the position is lost exactly where people spend most of the day: the US EPA National Human Activity Pattern Survey (9,386 respondents, 1992 to 1994) found 87% of time in enclosed buildings and about 6% in vehicles. Other countries and years differ, so use the number as an order of magnitude, not a constant. Indoor questions are also finer: which room, which shelf, which side of a door. There is no single indoor system, so each building brings its own infrastructure. The next slide asks when a location is worth having.
