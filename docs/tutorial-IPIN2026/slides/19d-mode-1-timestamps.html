This is how Channel Sounding turns the round-trip idea into numbers, in a step that exchanges packets. The initiator transmits a CS_SYNC packet; the reflector receives it one time of flight later, waits a fixed turnaround, and transmits its own CS_SYNC, which reaches the initiator one time of flight after that. Each device timestamps the departure of what it sends and the arrival of what it receives, at its antenna port, with known internal delays removed. An arrival time is not a detected edge: the receiver correlates the incoming packet with the access address it expects, and with the random sequence if there is one, and takes the delay where the correlation peaks. The initiator measures A, from its departure to its arrival; the reflector measures B, from its arrival to its departure. Each interval uses only one clock, so the unknown offset between the clocks never enters. The difference A minus B is the round trip, twice the time of flight. The initiator also corrects for the drift between the two clocks with the fractional frequency offset it measured in mode 0, which is why the equation divides by one plus FFO times ten to the minus six. The initiator's controller applies that correction itself, before it reports, and the reflector must not; so the host only subtracts the two reports, which is what our app does. Neither side reports raw timestamps: each reports its interval minus the known nominal part, the packet length and the interlude, as a signed number in half-nanosecond units; the half nanosecond is the reporting unit, not the accuracy. The RTT type in the configuration chooses what is correlated: the access address alone, a sounding sequence, or a random sequence. The sounding sequence alternates ones and zeros, so the GFSK frequency swings as fast as the modulation allows; that widens the effective bandwidth, sharpens the timing, and also yields phase. Because it is periodic, the receiver locks on the access address first and only then refines on the sequence. The random sequence is harder to spoof and comes with an attack-detection metric, NADM.
