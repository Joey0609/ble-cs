Scheduling answers where every step goes: which mode, which channel, at what time. The answer is never transmitted. After CS setup both controllers hold the same configuration, the same agreed timing and the same seeded CS random generator, and each runs the same rules, so the reflector knows what the initiator does next without being told. We build the schedule from the inside out: here how one subevent is filled, then, below, the mode sequence, how channels are selected, how subevents form CS events, and how events form a procedure.

A subevent is a time budget, the subevent length agreed for the procedure. It always opens with one to three mode-0 steps, which re-establish the shared frequency; their channels come from their own shuffled array, channel selection #3a. Next come up to three main-mode steps repeated from the end of the previous subevent, on the same channels; the first subevent of a procedure has nothing to repeat, and our firmware requests no repetition. Then the measurement: blocks of n main-mode steps, each followed by one sub-mode step. The two modes are set once in the CS configuration and stay the same for the whole procedure; what changes is n, which the random generator draws again for every block, between the configured minimum and maximum. Setting the minimum equal to the maximum gives a fixed pattern. These steps take their channels from the other array, #3b or #3c. Before every step after the first, the radios retune for T_FCS. A step is added only while it and its T_FCS still fit; then the subevent ends, with at most 160 steps.

The figure is to scale with the planner's step model: mode 2 with a mode-1 sub-mode, two mode-0 steps, LE 2M, T_IP 145 µs and T_FCS 150 µs, in the firmware's 6 ms subevent. Two mode-0 steps and eleven more fit in 5.715 ms; the next mode-2 step would need 473 µs with its T_FCS, and only 285 µs remain. The counts three, two and five are one possible draw from the firmware's range of 2 to 10. Only four steps of the block of five fit; the block does not restart at the subevent boundary, so its fifth step, then its sub-mode step, come first in the next subevent, right after the mode-0 steps. The planner uses a fixed count of two instead, which fits 14 steps in 5.96 ms, the procedure shown on the ACL timing slide.
