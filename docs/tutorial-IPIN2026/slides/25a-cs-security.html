How Channel Sounding answers each attack

AttackWhat CS does, and why it works
Amplified signal strengthMeasure phase and time, not strength. A stronger signal changes neither.
Relayed connectionTime the round trip (RTT). A relay can only add delay: the key looks farther, not nearer.
Shifted tone phaseSend tone extensions at random; cross-check with RTT. A tone where none was sent exposes the relay.
Predicted packetsRandomize every step. A generator shared only by the two peers picks channels and packet contents; attack detection flags altered packets.
Bluetooth defines four CS security levels, from basic ranging up to precise timing with attack detection, so each application can require the protection it needs.
Core 6.0 · Vol 6, Part H, §3.5; §4.4.1; §4.8·Vol 3, Part C, §10.11.1

Take the four weak points from the previous slide in turn. Signal strength: CS does not estimate distance from how strong the signal is, so amplifying it gains nothing. Relay: round-trip time is bounded by the speed of light, so any relay, cable or processing only adds delay and makes the key look farther. Phase: because phase is periodic, a relay could shift each tone to hide its delay. CS makes that hard in two ways. After each tone comes an extension slot that carries the tone or stays silent, chosen at random, and the receiver reports whether it heard a tone where none was expected. And the RTT from the same procedure gives an independent distance that the phase result must agree with.

All of this rests on unpredictability. Both controllers run a deterministic random bit generator, the DRBG, seeded with fresh material exchanged over the encrypted link during CS setup. It picks the channel order, access addresses, sounding-sequence markers or random payloads, antenna order and tone extensions, so the peers agree on every step while an outsider cannot anticipate one. With sounding or random sequences, the controller also compares each received packet with the expected waveform and reports a normalized attack detector metric (NADM), from "attack extremely unlikely" to "extremely likely".

Security levels: level 1 uses tones or RTT; level 2 adds 150 ns RTT accuracy with tones; level 3 tightens RTT to 10 ns; level 4 adds sounding or random sequences and NADM. Our firmware's default RTT type is access-address only, so it does not reach level 4. Keep two cautions. The protection depends on the pairing, the configured level and how the application uses the indicators; NADM is an indicator, not a proof. And security is not measurement quality: a low-noise range is not proof of security, and a secured exchange is not proof of line of sight.
