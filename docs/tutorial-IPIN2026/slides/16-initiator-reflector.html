The reflector is an active radio, not a mirror

LINK LAYER · PREREQUISITE

The two must share an encrypted Low Energy asynchronous connection (LE ACL): one is its Central, the other its Peripheral.

CS ROLES

Link Layer roles do not imply CS roles: the Central or the Peripheral can be the initiator.

HOST · APPLICATION ROLES

Advertising and data-service roles follow from connection setup and the application. CS does not depend on them.

Core 6.0 · Vol 6, Part H (CS)·Vol 6, Part B (Link Layer)

Start with the premise: the reflector is not a passive mirror. In every CS step it receives the initiator's tone or packet, measures it and transmits its own; an ordinary wall reflection is not the protocol reflector. Two active radios that must agree on every step's channel and timing need a link, so the prerequisite follows: the two share an established, encrypted LE ACL connection. In the Link Layer that connection has two roles: the Central sent the connection request, the Peripheral advertised and accepted it. Encryption comes from pairing or an earlier bond; a controller refuses to start CS security on an unencrypted link. The Link Layer roles do not imply the CS roles: either the Central or the Peripheral can be the initiator, so the reflector does not have to be connected by the initiator. The CS role is chosen per CS configuration, in its Role field. The Link Layer roles still appear inside CS setup, since the Central starts the CS security exchange, but they do not decide the CS roles. Above the Link Layer, GAP names follow from how the connection was made, and GATT client and server come from the application; CS does not depend on either. The connectionless GAP roles, Broadcaster and Observer, never connect and so cannot run CS. We meet the GATT roles again with the Ranging Service. Below (↓), the connection step by step.
