This is how Channel Sounding solves the problem, and it happens at the start of every subevent. In each of its opening steps the initiator sends a CS_SYNC and the reflector answers with a CS_SYNC followed by a tone. The initiator measures the frequency of that tone and turns it into the reflector's FFO, after taking out the reflector's FAE from the table it received before ranging started. The FFO of the first step sets the shared frequency for the rest of the subevent, f E equals f0 times one plus ten to the minus six FFO E. The FFO is signed, positive when the reflector runs fast, and f E then lies above the nominal channel. Every later step, from both radios, is transmitted there, tones within 10 kHz and CS_SYNC packets within 20 kHz. In practice the initiator moves its transmitter to the reflector's offset. The same estimate corrects round-trip times, which are divided by one plus the FFO, and because the phases are now measured at one shared frequency, the phase drift between the two tones is removed. What stays in the phases is only the error of that estimate, a fraction of a ppm with a sign that changes from subevent to subevent, not the offset itself. The initiator reports the offset it used for each subevent, in steps of 0.01 ppm, so the host can see it. The figure shows two opening steps; a subevent may open with one to three. It is illustrative, not to scale.
