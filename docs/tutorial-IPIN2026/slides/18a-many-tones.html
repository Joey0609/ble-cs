Fundamentals of phase-based ranging: many tones

Repeat the two-way exchange on every CS channel \( f_k \): 72 channels, 1 MHz apart, 2402–2480 MHz. Example: d = 5 m.

\( \Delta\Theta = \Theta(f_2) - \Theta(f_1) = -\dfrac{4\pi d}{c}\,\Delta f \)

\( \Rightarrow\ d = -\dfrac{c}{4\pi}\,\dfrac{\Delta\Theta}{\Delta f} \)

Offsets cancel; Θ falls with frequency: fit its slope

\( d_{\text{amb}} = \dfrac{c}{2\,\Delta f} \approx 150\ \text{m} \)

1 MHz spacing: ambiguity grows from 6 cm to 150 m

\( \dfrac{c}{2B} \approx 2\ \text{m} \)

Bandwidth B ≈ 74 MHz sets how well two paths separate
The fix is to repeat the same two-way measurement on many frequencies. The round-trip phase Theta is linear in frequency: minus 4 pi d f over c. It falls as frequency rises, because the same delay is more cycles of a faster tone: a straight line with slope minus 4 pi d over c, the same direction the app shows for measured data. Left: what is measured, wrapped into minus pi to pi. Right: after unwrapping across channels. The missing points near 2426 MHz are CS channels 23 to 25, which are never used; that 4 MHz gap limits safe adjacent-channel unwrapping to about 19 m. The distance comes from differences, not from one phase. Between two channels Delta f apart, the two-way phase changes by Delta Theta = minus 4 pi d Delta f over c, so d = minus c over 4 pi times Delta Theta over Delta f. Differencing cancels any phase offset common to both channels, and the unknown whole cycles of the single tone drop out as long as Delta Theta is unwrapped. With all channels, fit a straight line to the unwrapped phases; the derivative d Theta / d f is only the continuous limit of that slope. Minus the slope, over 2 pi, is the round-trip group delay: comparing phases at several frequencies recovers the delay that a single tone cannot show. With 1 MHz spacing the phase-vs-frequency pattern only repeats after 150 m. The total span of about 74 MHz sets the resolution for separating a direct path from reflections: about 2 m.
