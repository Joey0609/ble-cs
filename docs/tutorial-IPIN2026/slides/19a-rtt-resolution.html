Fundamentals of round-trip timing: resolution

\( d = \dfrac{c}{2}\,\mathrm{RTT} \): 6.7 ns of round trip per metre The arrival is the peak of the correlation with the known pattern

  • No ambiguity: nothing wraps around, however far
  • But coarse: 1 ns ↔ 15 cm, while the peak is ~870 ns wide

One packet: unambiguous but coarse → repeat it over many steps

The receiver finds the arrival by correlating the received packet with the pattern it expects and taking the peak. There is no wrap-around, so RTT is unambiguous: each metre adds 6.7 ns to the round trip. But the signal is only about 1 to 2 MHz wide, so the correlation peak is hundreds of nanoseconds wide, while 1 ns is 15 cm; the plot shows the peak moving by only 100 ns for 30 m. With the LE 2M PHY, a 96-bit sequence and 20 dB SNR the theoretical single-packet bound is about half a metre, and practical results need averaging over many steps, which the next slide takes up.
