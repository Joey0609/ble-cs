The budget starts from the smallest unit, one step. The step drawings earlier in the deck showed packets, tones and gaps. The gaps are not free choices of the application. Each controller reports in its capabilities which optional values it supports: T_IP1, the interlude inside a mode-0 or mode-1 step; T_IP2, the interlude inside a tone exchange of mode 2 or 3; T_FCS, the frequency-change time between steps; and T_PM, the phase-measurement time per antenna path. The values 145, 145, 150 and 40 µs are not in those capability lists because every controller supports them. The configuration may use only a value both peers support, and the controllers report the result in the configuration-complete event.

Our pair, an nRF54LM20 DK initiator with an nRF54L15 reflector, selected T_IP1 30, T_IP2 20 and T_FCS 60 µs; T_PM is 10 µs unless a SoftDevice Controller option selects 40. The planner then gives 278 µs for a mode-0 step and 138 µs for a mode-2 step, each with its retune gap, so 72 channels and two mode-0 steps take 10.4 ms. With the mandatory values a mode-2 step and its gap take 473 µs, and the same work takes 34.9 ms: a 6 ms subevent holds 12 steps instead of 41. In the lab, T_PM 40 instead of 10 cut a 5 ms subevent from 36 steps to 20 (the planner predicts 34 and 19); with a 20 ms subevent all 74 steps fitted at every T_PM. Longer tones can lower phase noise, but the controller then fits fewer channels into the same time. A peer that supports only the mandatory values therefore changes the interval you need and the rate you get.
