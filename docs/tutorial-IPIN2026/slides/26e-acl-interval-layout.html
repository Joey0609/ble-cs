The ACL interval decides how a procedure is laid out

The same 72 channels at three intervals: CS events and RAS results take turns in the ACL intervals

Rate = 1 / (procedure interval × ACL interval), and the procedure interval must hold the CS events and the RAS events

The ACL connection interval is the clock of the whole schedule. CS events start at an offset of at least 500 µs after an ACL anchor, and the CS event interval and the procedure interval are counted in whole connection intervals. The specification lets a CS event, and even one subevent, run past the next ACL anchor. Our controller does not: the SoftDevice Controller refused a procedure whose CS event left less than about 1 ms of the interval (HCI 0x20 at procedure enable), and every accepted run left at least 1 ms. That observation is not yet isolated (implementation_plan.md §3.3), and other controllers may allow more. The figure puts the baseline of the previous slides on the ACL clock: 72 channels once, mode 2, two mode-0 steps per subevent, one antenna path, and the step timings our nRF54 pair selected (T_IP1 30, T_IP2 20, T_FCS 60, T_PM 10 µs), 10.4 ms of steps. At 7.5 ms that does not fit in one CS event: a 6 ms subevent holds 41 steps, so the procedure needs two CS events and two more mode-0 steps. At 15 ms and 30 ms it fits in one 12 ms subevent. After the procedure, the reflector's results travel as RAS notifications in ACL events that carry no CS. At ATT MTU 498 on LE 1M they need about 8 ms of airtime: two ACL events at 7.5 ms, one at 15 or 30 ms. The next procedure can start only after those events, so the procedure interval is 4 at 7.5 ms, 2 at 15 ms and 2 at 30 ms: 30, 30 and 60 ms. The shortest interval is not the fastest, and the longest halves the rate. Treat the procedure interval here as a floor. In our lab runs, a single ACL event between procedures lost the RAS data of 0.5 to 1.5 percent of procedures; the hostless initiator's default therefore uses a 17.5 ms interval, one 16 ms subevent, and a procedure every 3 intervals (52.5 ms), so RAS gets two events.
