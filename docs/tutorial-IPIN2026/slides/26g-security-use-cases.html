The security slides showed what Channel Sounding can do against amplification, relays, phase shifting and prediction. Which of it a product needs is a separate decision, and it depends on the use case. Start from what a wrong distance would cost and who would gain from causing it. A research or sports-tracking system such as SensWear loses data quality, and nobody gains from faking it, so tones alone, security level 1, are enough and the airtime is better spent on channels and antenna paths. Indoor navigation and asset tracking can mislead a user, so an RTT cross-check that catches gross phase errors, level 2, is reasonable. Proximity convenience features, such as auto-locking a laptop or a find-my alert, benefit from 10 ns RTT, level 3. Access and payment decisions are the relay attacker's target: they need level 4, RTT with a sounding or random sequence, and an application that actually acts on the attack-detection metric NADM. This mapping is a suggestion; car-key, lock and payment ecosystems set their own requirements.

The levels come from the Generic Access Profile. Level 1 uses CS tones or CS RTT; level 2 needs 150 ns RTT accuracy with tones, about 22 m of distance; level 3 needs 10 ns, about 1.5 m; level 4 adds the sounding or random sequence and the NADM requirements.

Security also spends the budget of the previous slides. RTT needs mode 1 or mode 3 steps, and a longer RTT sequence lengthens every one of them: with our step timings, 72 channels in mode 3 take 18.2 ms with access-address RTT, 22.8 ms with a 32-bit sounding sequence and 32.0 ms with a 96-bit one. And the controllers must support it: our nRF54 SoftDevice Controller rejected the 32-bit sounding RTT type at configuration (HCI 0x2090, status 0x11), and the 96-bit and random types are untested, so this setup runs access-address RTT and does not reach level 4. Check both controllers' capabilities before promising a level.
