Two quantities: FFO and FAE

FFO · FRACTIONAL FREQUENCY OFFSET

How far a tone is off, in ppm

\( \mathrm{FFO}[k] = 10^{6}\,\dfrac{f[k] - f_0[k]\,(1 + 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{FAE}[k])}{f_0[k]\,(1 + 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{FAE}[k])} \)

The measured frequency f[k] of step k’s tone against where it should be, as a fraction of the carrier. Measured on every mode-0 step; |FFO| < 50 parts per million (ppm).

FAE · FREQUENCY ACTUATION ERROR

How far a radio misses a channel

A frequency synthesizer turns the crystal reference into each channel frequency with finite resolution, so on channel k it realizes f0[k](1 + 10−6·FAE[k]): a small, repeatable miss, in parts per million.

The reflector’s tone carries its FAE and the crystal offset ε: \( f[k] \approx f_0[k]\,(1 + 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{FAE}[k])\,(1 + \varepsilon) \), so \( \mathrm{FFO}[k] \approx 10^{6}\,\varepsilon \): ε in ppm, with the FAE taken out.
FFO measures ε, which changes with every crystal and temperature, so it is measured every subevent; FAE is a radio property, retrieved as a table only when needed (see slide 23b).
Core · Vol 6, Part A, §3.5.1 (fractional frequency offset)

Two quantities, and they are easy to confuse. The fractional frequency offset, FFO, is an observation: how far the frequency of a tone is from where it should be, divided by the carrier and expressed in parts per million. The specification defines it for each mode-0 step k: the measured frequency f of k, minus the nominal channel frequency corrected by the FAE, over that corrected frequency, times ten to the six. It must stay below 50 ppm, and the initiator reports the value it used in steps of 0.01 ppm. The frequency actuation error, FAE, is a property of the reflector's radio. Its local oscillator is synthesized: a phase-locked loop or a numerically controlled oscillator turns the crystal into each channel frequency, but with finite resolution, the divider ratios or the frequency steps it can make. On some channels the frequency it actually realizes misses the nominal one by a small, repeatable amount. Because it is repeatable, the reflector can publish it as a table, one value per channel, 72 in all, between minus 4 and plus 3.97 ppm. If No_FAE is 0 and no table is already known, the initiator retrieves that table before ranging starts and takes it out of every FFO it measures, so a synthesizer quirk is not mistaken for crystal offset. A radio that realizes every channel exactly has an FAE of zero; a peer declaring No_FAE = 1 must not be sent a table request. So how do the two relate to epsilon, the relative crystal offset of the previous slide? The initiator measures the reflector's tone on its own clock, with its own synthesizer error already compensated. The tone then arrives at f0 times one plus the FAE in ppm, times one plus epsilon: the synthesizer's miss, scaled by the crystal offset. Put that into the definition and the FAE cancels: FFO is epsilon, in parts per million, to first order. Positive means the reflector's crystal runs fast against the initiator's. FAE is the part of the offset that is not the crystal, which is why it has to be removed before the FFO is used as epsilon. The bar chart is an illustration, not a real table.
