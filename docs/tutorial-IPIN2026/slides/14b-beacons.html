Beacons: signal strength mapped to range

SINCE
Apple iBeacon (2013); also Eddystone, AltBeacon
LINK
None: the beacon advertises an identifier and its measured power at 1 m
MODEL
Received signal strength indicator (RSSI) = P1m − 10·n·log10 d, solved for d
RESULT
Zones (immediate, near, far), or a coarse position from several beacons
LIMITATIONS
  • 6 dB of error doubles or halves the range (n = 2)
  • Fading, the body and orientation move RSSI by several dB
  • n and P1m change with the room and the receiver
  • The advertised identifier is public: easy to spoof or replay
Apple · iBeacon|Determining the proximity to an iBeacon device

Beacons are connectionless: a beacon advertises an identifier and a measured power, the RSSI a receiver should see at 1 m. The receiver compares its RSSI with that value and solves a log- distance path-loss model for the distance. The plot uses P1m = −59 dBm and n = 2, a teaching example rather than a measurement. Because distance sits inside a logarithm, a dB error becomes a multiplicative range error: with n = 2, 6 dB doubles or halves the distance, so a reading of −71 dBm fits anything from 2 to 8 m. Fading, a hand or body between the devices, antenna orientation and phone-to-phone RSSI calibration all move the reading by several dB. That is why Core Location reports a proximity zone (immediate, near, far) with only a rough accuracy estimate. Several beacons give a coarse position by trilateration or by fingerprinting a site survey. The identifier is broadcast in the clear, so it can be copied or replayed.
