72 channels take 10.4 ms with our step timings, so one CS event needs ≥ 12.5 ms; with the mandatory timings, 34.9 ms and ≥ 36.25 ms.

Start from the procedure, then pick the shortest interval that holds it in one CS event on your controller.
Core 6.0 · Vol 6, Part B, §4.5; §4.5.18·Planner model and lab runs

The rates come from the same planner workload as the previous figure: 72 channels once, mode 2, two mode-0 steps per subevent, the step timings our nRF54 pair selected, and RAS at ATT MTU 498 on LE 1M. With those timings the whole procedure takes 10.4 ms. Our controller ends a CS event about 1 ms before the next anchor, although the specification allows longer, so 12.5 ms is the shortest interval that holds it in one CS event; its RAS data, about 8 ms, then fits in the next ACL event, and procedures repeat every 2 intervals, 25 ms, 40 per second. At 7.5 ms the procedure needs two CS events and two RAS events, every 4 intervals, 30 ms. At 15 ms it is every 2 intervals, 30 ms; at 30 ms, every 60 ms.

A shorter interval also costs energy when nothing is measured: the Central opens an ACL event at every anchor, so the radio wakes every 7.5 ms instead of every 30 ms. A longer interval leaves room for more steps in one CS event, but results and control messages wait longer. The Core 6.0 baseline allows 7.5 ms to 4 s in 1.25 ms units; the project defaults to 7.5 ms, and the hostless initiator's fallback uses 17.5 ms with one 16 ms subevent. The interval arrives in CONNECT_IND; this project's initiator connects with the interval of its CS configuration and leaves automatic parameter updates off. Either side can later ask for other timing with the Connection Parameters Request procedure (LL_CONNECTION_PARAM_REQ/RSP, then LL_CONNECTION_UPDATE_IND from the Central), and every CS timing derived from the interval changes with it, so plan the procedure for the interval the link actually has. With the timings every controller must support (T_IP1 and T_IP2 145, T_FCS 150, T_PM 40 µs) the same 72 channels take 34.9 ms, and the threshold moves to 36.25 ms: the peer's capabilities matter as much as your own.
