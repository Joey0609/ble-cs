Why ranging needs security: distance opens doors

ACCESS
Car keys, smart locks and door badges open when the key is near: the range decides who gets in.
CRYPTO
Pairing and encryption prove who is talking, not where. A relay forwards the encrypted packets unchanged.
SHOWN
Keyless cars relayed over 50 m (2011); BLE phone keys and smart locks (2022), adding only 8 ms.
GOAL
The attacker wants far to look near: a distance-reduction attack.
WHAT AN ATTACKER EXPLOITS
  • Signal strength: amplify it, and far looks near
  • A connection: relay it, encryption intact
  • Phase: periodic, so a relay can hide its delay
  • Predictable packets: answer them before they arrive
Francillon, Danev, Čapkun · NDSS 2011·NCC Group · BLE relay advisory, 2022·Ólafsdóttir, Ranganathan, Čapkun · CHES 2017

Before the mechanisms, ask why a ranging standard needs security at all. Many applications that motivate Channel Sounding act on the distance: a car unlocks when its digital key is within a metre or two, a smart lock opens when the phone reaches the door, a laptop stays unlocked while the watch is near. The distance is then an access decision, and an attacker gains from one kind of error: making a far key look near, a distance-reduction attack. Ordinary link security does not help. Pairing, authentication and encryption prove who is at the other end of the link, not where it is. The figure shows the relay attack: one relay waits near the car, another near the owner, who is indoors and far away. They forward the radio traffic, over any distance and without decrypting it, so every cryptographic check passes.

This is not hypothetical. Francillon, Danev and Čapkun relayed the passive keyless entry systems of 10 car models from 8 manufacturers in 2011, with the key up to 50 m away. In 2022 NCC Group relayed Bluetooth LE at the link layer, adding as little as 8 ms of round-trip latency, unlocked and operated a Tesla Model 3 and Model Y with a phone key, and opened smart locks; their recommendation was secure time-of-flight ranging. Signal strength is no defence, because an amplifier makes a far signal strong. Phase alone is not either: it is periodic, so a relay can shift each tone to hide its delay, and Ólafsdóttir, Ranganathan and Čapkun made a multi-carrier phase-ranging system report under 3 m with the devices more than 50 m apart.

What physics does give is a bound on time: no relay beats the speed of light, so a relay can only add delay, provided the attacker cannot predict a packet and answer early. That is the requirement Channel Sounding takes on: timing evidence that a relay can only lengthen, and exchanges that nobody outside the link can predict. The next slide shows how.
