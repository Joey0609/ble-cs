Many steps: unambiguous and decimetre-level → the anchor for the precise but ambiguous phase

One packet gives a coarse round trip, so Channel Sounding repeats the exchange: a procedure holds many steps, each on its own hopped channel, and each step gives one independent round-trip time. Left: 32 steps for a device 5 m away, plotted against channel as the app's RTT tab shows them. Two failed the packet check and are rejected; the other 30 scatter by about half a metre, and a few sit well above the truth. A delay is the same quantity on every channel, so unlike phase the steps combine directly, with no unwrapping. Only the transmit and receive chain delay differs a little from channel to channel, so its calibration is per channel. Noise averages: M independent steps shrink the scatter by the square root of M. The bound for the LE 2M PHY with a 96-bit sequence at 20 dB SNR is 0.49 m for one step and 8.6 cm for 32. Bias does not average. Right: 1 ns left in the chain delay is 15 cm on every step; beyond about ten steps it is larger than the noise, so calibration buys more than more steps. The clock-rate error is a bias of the same kind: the reflector's turnaround, timed on its own crystal, is stretched the same way on every step, and only the mode-0 frequency offset removes it. Hopping helps against multipath. On one channel a reflection shifts every step the same way; across channels its phase relative to the direct path changes, so part of that error turns into scatter. What is left mostly pulls long, so a robust summary matters: here the mean is 5.30 m and the median 5.15 m. A low quantile goes further. Our app does exactly this much, per procedure: it pairs the initiator and reflector steps, keeps a pair when both report a time difference and passed the access-address check (a bit-error limit is optional), and shows the accepted count, mean, median and spread. It applies no per-channel calibration and no quantile. Averaged RTT is unambiguous and, once calibrated, good to decimetres: the anchor that tells the phase estimate which ambiguity interval it is in, while the phase supplies the precision.
