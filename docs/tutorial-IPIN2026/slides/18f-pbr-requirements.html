What phase-based ranging requires

01 · TWO-WAY PHASE

Both directions, one channel

Initiator to reflector, then back, within one step. Each radio keeps its oscillator phase-continuous, so φI and φR cancel.

02 · PHASE EXCHANGE

Both phases in one place

The reflector reports its phases over the ACL link, or folds them into its reply tone (inline PCT transfer, Core 6.3).

03 · FREQUENCY

A known frequency offset

Both tune to the same channel; mode 0 measures what is left between their oscillators, or it becomes range bias.

04 · TIMING

A shared step schedule

Both know, to the microsecond, when to send and when to listen, how far apart the two tones are, and which antenna path each tone uses.

Also assumed: a channel that stays still during the step, and calibrated hardware phase offsets.

The one-tone equation hides four requirements. First, two-way phase: the tone goes both ways on the same channel within one step, and each radio must keep its local oscillator phase- continuous between its transmission and its reception, or correct for it; the specification states this directly. Only then do the two oscillator phases cancel. Second, the two phases must meet. Normally the reflector reports its measured phase, as a phase correction term per step and antenna path, and the initiator combines it with its own; in this project that report travels over the ACL link through the Ranging Service. Core 6.3 adds inline PCT transfer: the reflector turns its reply tone by the phase it measured, so the initiator directly sees the doubled channel phase. Third, frequency: both radios tune to the same channel, but their oscillators still differ slightly; mode-0 steps measure that residual offset, because any offset left over rotates the phase between the two tones and becomes a range bias; the calibration section puts a number on it. Fourth, timing: both radios must follow the same step schedule to the microsecond, knowing when to transmit and when to listen, and the time between the two tones must be known to correct the frequency error; with several antennas, both must also follow the same antenna path order, or phases from different paths mix. Underneath all four: the channel must not change during the step, so no fast motion, and the hardware phase offsets of each radio must be calibrated.
