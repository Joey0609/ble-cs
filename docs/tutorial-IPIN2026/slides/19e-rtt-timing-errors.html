Every error in time becomes an error in range at half the speed of light: one nanosecond of round-trip error is 15 cm. Two system effects dominate. The first is the clock rate: the reflector measures its interval, about 176 microseconds for a mode-1 step on LE 2M with the planner's 145 microsecond T_IP1, on its own crystal. A 12 ppm offset stretches that interval by 2.1 ns, which is 32 cm. The form is c times epsilon times the time between the two observations, over two, and the same offset biases phase-based ranging in the same way, as the calibration section shows; the initiator removes it by dividing by one plus the fractional frequency offset measured in mode 0. The second is the hardware: the transmit and receive chains add delay between the antenna and the point where the timestamp is taken. The specification requires devices to compensate it, but any residual shows up as a constant bias; 10 ns uncalibrated is 1.5 m, so calibrate on a known distance. The numbers are illustrative magnitudes, not measurements.
