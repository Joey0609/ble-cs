Direction Finding: bearings from antenna arrays

SINCE
Bluetooth 5.1 (2019)
SIGNAL
The tag appends a Constant Tone Extension (CTE) to its packets
MEASURE
Each locator samples in-phase and quadrature (I/Q) across its antennas; the phase step gives angle θ
RESULT
Angle of arrival: one bearing per locator; two or more intersect at the tag
LIMITATIONS
  • Needs fixed locators, each with an array, RF switch and calibration
  • An angle is not a range: one locator gives a line, not a point
  • Reflections bias the angle; 1° is 17 cm at 10 m
  • Array layout and polarization must be known and stable
Bluetooth SIG · Direction Finding|Direction Finding technical overview

Direction Finding, added in Bluetooth 5.1, moves from amplitude to phase. The tag broadcasts packets with a Constant Tone Extension, a stretch of unmodulated tone. Each fixed locator switches across the antennas of its array while sampling I/Q; the phase step between neighbouring antennas depends on the angle of arrival. Each locator therefore gives a bearing, a line, and two or more bearings intersect at the tag. That is angle of arrival, AoA; in angle of departure, AoD, the array transmits and the phone computes its own direction, which needs the array layout. The cost is infrastructure: arrays, RF switches, mounting and calibration. Reflections bias the angle, and a fixed angle error grows into a larger position error with distance: 1 degree is about 17 cm at 10 m. The angle comes from phase across space; Channel Sounding uses phase across frequency.
