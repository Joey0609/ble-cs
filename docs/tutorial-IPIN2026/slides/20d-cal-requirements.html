What frequency calibration requires

01 · FRESH MEASUREMENT

Measure again every subevent

One to three mode-0 steps open every subevent: the offset drifts with temperature and time.

02 · KNOWN FAE

The reflector’s table first

The initiator reads the FAE table before ranging starts, or uses a cached copy.

03 · STABLE CRYSTAL

An offset that holds still

Within a subevent, the mode-0 estimates must agree to 1 ppm, and |FFO| must stay below 50 ppm.

04 · SHARED FREQUENCY

Both radios at fE

Every later step is sent at the shared frequency: tones within 10 kHz, CS_SYNC within 20 kHz.

Frequency calibration does not remove chain delays or hardware phase offsets: those need their own calibration.
Core · Vol 6, Part A, §3.5

Four conditions make frequency calibration work, all from the specification. It must be fresh: the crystals drift with temperature and time, so every subevent opens with one to three mode-0 steps instead of reusing an old value. The FAE must be known: the initiator reads the reflector's table before ranging starts, or uses a copy it cached from an earlier connection. The crystal must be stable: within one subevent the mode-0 estimates must agree to within 1 ppm of the first, and the offset must stay below 50 ppm. And the frequency must be shared: every later step is transmitted at the shared frequency, tones within 10 kHz and CS_SYNC packets within 20 kHz of it. What frequency calibration does not fix: the fixed delays in each radio's transmit and receive chains, and the fixed phase offsets of its hardware and antenna paths. Those are separate calibrations, done once per device or on a known distance, and they matter as much, as the round-trip error slide showed.
