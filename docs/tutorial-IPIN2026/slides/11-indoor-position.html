Indoors, a position is solved from ranges

A useful answer is more than a dot: where, when, and how certain?

Range changes a signal's amplitude, phase and delay. Each can be turned into a range.

Each anchor has a known position in a local coordinate frame and measures a range to the node; the position is solved from those ranges. The measured ranges carry error, so the estimate is a region, drawn as the uncertainty ellipse. A useful answer also needs an update rate and that uncertainty, not only a dot. Where does a range come from? Distance changes three properties of the radio signal between the two devices: its amplitude, which weakens with distance; its carrier phase, which turns one full cycle for every wavelength travelled, about 12 cm at 2.4 GHz; and its delay, about 3.3 ns for every metre. Any of the three can be turned into a range: signal strength, phase and time of flight are the three families we will meet. The next slide shows why indoors none of them is as simple as it looks.
