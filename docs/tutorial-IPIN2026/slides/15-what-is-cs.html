What is Channel Sounding in Bluetooth 6?

A Bluetooth Core 6.0 feature that lets two connected LE devices measure the distance between them — precisely and securely.

  • Two roles on an LE connection: the initiator starts and measures, the reflector measures and answers
  • Frequency hopping over up to 72 channels, 1 MHz apart, from 2402 to 2480 MHz
  • Two ranging methods
    • Phase-based ranging (PBR): tone phase vs. frequency
    • RTT: round-trip time of packets
  • Security built in: random channel order and bit sequences, with checks against relay and spoofing attacks
  • Up to 4 antenna paths to handle multipath
\( d = \dfrac{c}{4\pi}\left|\dfrac{\Delta\varphi}{\Delta f}\right| \) \( d = \dfrac{c}{2}\left(t_{\text{round}} - t_{\text{turnaround}}\right) \)
Channel Sounding (CS) arrived in Bluetooth Core 6.0 (2024); this project follows Core v6.3. It runs over an existing LE ACL connection: the two sides agree on a CS configuration, then run CS procedures made of subevents and steps. The initiator and reflector exchange tones and packets on up to 72 channels (CS channels 2–22 and 26–76, 1 MHz apart). Channel order and packet contents come from a shared random generator, so an attacker cannot predict them. PBR: each side measures the phase of the other's tone. The combined round-trip phase falls linearly with frequency, with slope −4πd/c, so the slope across channels gives the distance. With 1 MHz spacing the result repeats every 150 m. RTT: the initiator times a packet's round trip and subtracts the reflector's known turnaround time. This is coarser than PBR but not ambiguous, and it helps detect relay attacks. PBR can measure up to four antenna paths to average out multipath; an RTT packet uses one antenna. Initiator, reflector and the link they need are on the next slide; step modes 0–3 come later.
