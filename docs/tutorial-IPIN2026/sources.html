SensWear · From Channel Sounding to Ring Tracks

Source and asset notes. Prepared 27 September 2026. Presentation

Scope

The presentation contains 49 main slides with 90 minutes of allocated delivery time, including prediction pauses and app demonstrations, plus six optional appendix slides. The conference page describes a 180-minute event; this deck covers its theoretical first part and introduces the practical second part. It is a teaching artifact, not a report of a newly measured localization experiment.

Screenshot provenance

The four images/app-*.png screenshots were rendered from the supplied PyQt6 application's normal widgets. Planner images contain illustrative configurations. Controller and session images use the built-in simulator; the PBR image uses synthetic packets from python/tests/test_results.py to show the current four-plot Results view, including the IFFT range profile. These are not measured RF captures or evidence of distance accuracy.

An isolated capture script needed an explicit base-class dispatch for ConnectionParametersPacket.from_frame because the inspected slotted dataclass's zero-argument super() failed under the capture environment. The small runtime-only workaround preserved normal packet decoding and the legacy branch. It did not alter source files, observations or estimator behavior. The source application was left unchanged.

Original mathematical demonstrations

  • RTT: elapsed initiator time equals chosen reflector turnaround plus 2d/c. The display subtracts turnaround and computes the corrected round trip.
  • PBR: a reciprocal single-path complex channel, optionally with an echo of relative amplitude 0.42 and 4 m excess path. The squared complex channel models the two-way product; sorted phases are unwrapped and fitted with a straight line. This is an illustrative model, without noise, RF calibration or hardware validation.
  • Geometry: a 12 m × 8 m floor plane and fixed target at (6, 4) m. The local covariance is σ²(JᵀJ)⁻¹ for independent unbiased range noise with σ = 0.25 m. Ellipse axes are one-standard-deviation principal axes, drawn to room scale; no empirical confidence guarantee is asserted.
